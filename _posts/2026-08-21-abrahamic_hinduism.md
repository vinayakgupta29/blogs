### Bhagavad Gītā 18.63 

> **इति ते ज्ञानमाख्यातं गुह्याद्गुह्यतरं मया ।  
> विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥ १८.६३ ॥**
> 
> _iti te jñānam ākhyātaṃ guhyād guhyataraṃ mayā_  
> _vimṛśyaitad aśeṣeṇa yathecchasi tathā kuru_

**Meaning:**

> “Thus I have explained to you this knowledge, which is more secret than the most secret. **Having fully reflected upon it, then do as you wish.**”

### Bhagavad Gītā 4.34 — You are told to **ask questions**

> **तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया ।  
> उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ ४.३४ ॥**
> 
> _tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā_  
> _upadekṣyanti te jñānaṃ jñāninas tattvadarśinaḥ_

**Meaning:**

> “Know this through respectful approach, **through questioning**, and through service. Those who know the truth and have realized it will teach you knowledge.”

### Bhagavad Gītā 4.39 — Faith is connected to gaining **knowledge**

This verse is important because it prevents us from going too far in the opposite direction.

> **श्रद्धावाँल्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः ।  
> ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥ ४.३९ ॥**
> 
> _śraddhāvān labhate jñānaṃ tatparaḥ saṃyatendriyaḥ_  
> _jñānaṃ labdhvā parāṃ śāntim acireṇādhigacchati_

**Meaning:**

> “The person endowed with faith, devoted and self-controlled, obtains knowledge; having obtained knowledge, he quickly attains supreme peace.”

### Bhagavad Gītā 16.24 — Determine what should and shouldn't be done

This one is especially relevant to your question about **actions**:

> **तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ ।  
> ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि ॥ १६.२४ ॥**
> 
> _tasmāc chāstraṃ pramāṇaṃ te kāryākārya-vyavasthitau_

**Meaning:**

> “Therefore, scripture is the authority for determining what ought to be done and what ought not to be done. **Having understood** the teachings of scripture, you should act accordingly.”

### Taittirīya Upaniṣad — Don't neglect study

The Taittirīya Upaniṣad gives the student a series of instructions after Vedic education:

> **सत्यान्न प्रमदितव्यम् ।  
> धर्मान्न प्रमदितव्यम् ।  
> स्वाध्यायप्रवचनाभ्यां न प्रमदितव्यम् ॥**
> 
> _satyān na pramaditavyam_  
> _dharmān na pramaditavyam_  
> _svādhyāya-pravacanābhyāṃ na pramaditavyam_

Among other things:

> **“Do not neglect truth. Do not neglect dharma. Do not neglect the study and teaching of the Veda.”**

### Manusmṛti 12.106 — Logic/reasoning has a place in understanding dharma

This is particularly interesting because you specifically mentioned **Smṛti**.

In the commentary on Manusmṛti 12.106, _tarka_ (reasoning/logic) is discussed in connection with philosophical systems such as Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika. The commentary explains that reasoning is relevant in understanding the subtle aspects of dharma, while also placing it within the larger authority structure of Vedic tradition.
### Rig Veda 1.89.1 — Seek good/noble ideas from _everywhere_

One of the most famous Vedic verses is:

> **आ नो भद्राः क्रतवो यन्तु विश्वतः ।**
> 
> _ā no bhadrāḥ kratavo yantu viśvataḥ_

The fuller mantra says:

> **आ नो भद्राः क्रतवो यन्तु विश्वतोऽदब्धासोऽपरीतास उद्भिदः ।  
> देवा नो यथा सदमिद् वृधेऽसन्नप्रायुवो रक्षितारो दिवेदिवे ॥**

A common translation is:

> **“May noble/auspicious thoughts or intentions come to us from every direction.**

### The Mahābhārata states the statement:

> **अचिन्त्याः खलु ये भावा  
> न तांस्तर्केण योजयेत् ।**

> _acintyāḥ khalu ye bhāvā  
> na tāṃs tarkeṇa yojayet_

Roughly:

### Mahābhārata — Kauśika: knowing a rule without understanding dharma

In the _Karna Parva_, the story of the sage Kauśika is told.

Kauśika had taken a vow to **always tell the truth**. One day, some innocent people fleeing from murderous robbers hid in the forest. The robbers came to Kauśika and asked him where the people had gone.

Because Kauśika had a simplistic understanding of his vow — _“I must always tell the truth”_ — he told them.

The robbers found and killed the people.

Mahabharata states  (8.49.46):
> तेनाधर्मेण महता वाग्दुरुक्तेन कौशिकः ।  
> गतः सुकष्टं नरकं सूक्ष्मधर्मेष्वकोविदः ।  
> अप्रभूतश्रुतो मूढो धर्माणामविभागवित् ॥ ४६ ॥
>
> _tenādharmeṇa mahatā vāgduruktena kauśikaḥ_ |  
> _gataḥ sukaṣṭaṃ narakaṃ sūkṣmadharmeṣvakovidaḥ_ |  
> _aprabhūtaśruto mūḍho dharmāṇāmavibhāgavit_ || 46 ||

`Due to that great unrighteous action (adharma) in speaking (truth that led to harm), Kauśika, who was unskilled in subtle matters of natural law (dharma), went to a very difficult hell. He was ignorant, foolish, and unable to discern the proper distinctions of natural laws (dharma).`
